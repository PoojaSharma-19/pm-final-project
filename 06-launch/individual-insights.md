## Friction points

_The biggest challenge was translating a broad business opportunity into a focused MVP. There were many potential features for the Riverty Mastercard, but prioritising only those that directly solved Lena's core problem—being unable to use Riverty immediately in-store—required difficult trade-offs. It was also challenging to define realistic experimentation parameters, such as the baseline, MDE, and sample size, because the case study intentionally provided assumptions rather than historical data. Balancing customer value, technical feasibility, and business goals while avoiding scope creep was the most demanding part of the project._

_____

## Key learnings

_One of the biggest lessons from this course was that successful products start with a clearly defined customer problem rather than a feature idea. I learned how qualitative research, quantitative metrics, experimentation, prioritisation, PRDs, and GTM planning all connect to form a complete product strategy. I also realised that a strong Product Manager must be comfortable making assumptions, validating them through experiments, and making evidence-based decisions instead of relying on intuition alone._

_____

## Aha! moment

_My biggest "aha" moment was understanding that product success is driven by customer behaviour, not feature delivery. Initially, I focused on adding attractive features, but through the course I realised that the MVP should validate a single hypothesis. For the Riverty Mastercard, the real objective was not simply launching a virtual card—it was enabling customers to make their first purchase immediately after approval and measuring whether that changed their behaviour. That shift in thinking fundamentally changed how I approached product design, prioritisation, experimentation, and go-to-market planning._
