# Problem Hook & Value Proposition (Module 1)

## Responses
- **Chosen path, which scenario are you committing to? (StreamLine or RouteLogic, or your own):** My Own - Riverty's First Consumer Card
- **Strategic crisis, based on the brief, if the company does nothing for 12 months, what happens to its market position?:** Nothing collapses in 12 months, but Riverty's position weakens slowly.-
1. Klarna pulls further ahead
2. Heavy users and interest income go elsewhere
3. The card slips further
- **Moment of misery, what is the user likely doing outside the product (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor) because your tool isn’t serving them?:** Customer-
1. Using Klarna or PayPal instead
2. Paying by existing debit or credit card
Merchant-
Checking conversion and cost-of-acceptance numbers in their own spreadsheets or dashboards to decide whether Riverty deserves checkout placement over Klarna or PayPal.
- **Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.:** Users with no financial cushion juggle scattered BNPL payments across apps they don't prefer, and every month Riverty waits to launch a card, Klarna and PayPal tighten their hold on that habit and Riverty stays just a checkout option instead of an everyday relationship.
- **Value proposition, based on the proposed initiative (Spotlight or Velocity), what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?:** Users can "keep it first, pay later" anywhere, in stores and beyond partner merchants, with one place to see what's due and settle on time. Riverty gets a direct, everyday relationship with its users instead of being just a checkout option.

Why launch now:

Regulation: launching now gets an exemption from the CCD2 rules.
Klarna: it's ahead in Germany and builds card habits every month.
Platform: the Mastercard and Paymentology platform is already paid for and earns nothing yet.
Capacity: the Amazon Business pilot (October 2026) absorbs capacity, so the card slips without a committed slot.

## Cold-read your own hook
- **Is the business risk high enough to justify a new initiative, a high-stakes threat, or a minor inconvenience?:** A strategic threat, not a crisis. It's too slow-burning to be high-stakes in 12 months, but too structural (replaceable checkout option, ~6% brand preference, Klarna ahead) to be a minor inconvenience. It justifies a focused initiative, not an all-in bet.
- **Is the moment of misery a systemic problem or just an edge case?:** It follows from Riverty's model: no in-store presence, availability only at partner merchants, and Lena choosing whatever appears at checkout. It affects every user, not a few.
- **Does the value proposition actually remove the obstacle you identified, or is it a generic feature?:** Partly. "Pay anywhere" removes the in-store and partner-only limits, but on its own it's generic, since Klarna Card and PayPal offer the same.
