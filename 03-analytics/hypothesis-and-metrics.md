- **Role , who you are solving for (from M2):** A 32-year-old salaried employee in Cologne on a middling income with no real cushion, who shops mostly on her phone.
- **Goal , what this user is ultimately trying to achieve:** To pay only after she has decided to keep the item.
- **Friction / moment of misery , the specific pain blocking their goal:** In a shop, Riverty isn't offered, so she pays by debit and the money leaves her account immediately, before she knows whether she'll keep what she bought.
- **Current workaround , the external tool or manual process they rely on (M2):** Klarna (app and checkout): the brief says she has it installed and uses it whenever it appears at checkout. This is the one most strongly supported. PayPal (Pay in 3 / Pay in 30): also installed, and used as a fallback BNPL option. Also supported by the brief. Her debit card: the likely forced choice in a shop, where Riverty isn't offered. The brief implies it, but doesn't state it. Her phone browser and online shops: where she would buy later if she skips the in-store purchase
- **Problem Hook , your one-sentence framing of the business crisis (M1):** Riverty's most loyal users carry Klarna and PayPal for everything outside its merchant base — and every month without a card is a month those habits harden around someone else's brand, in the market where the gap is already widest.
- **Value Proposition , the outcome your initiative promised to deliver (M1):** Users can "keep it first, pay later" anywhere, in stores and beyond partner merchants, with one place to see what's due and settle on time. Riverty gets a direct, everyday relationship with its users instead of being just a checkout option.
Why launch now:

Regulation: launching now gets an exemption from the CCD2 rules. Klarna: it's ahead in Germany and builds card habits every month. Platform: the Mastercard and Paymentology platform is already paid for and earns nothing yet. Capacity: the Amazon Business pilot (October 2026) absorbs capacity, so the card slips without a committed slot.

## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** Her stated preference: she "prefers the money to leave her account only after she has decided to keep the item.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** Lena, a 32-year-old salaried employee with limited savings, represents consumers who value payment flexibility and financial control. In Germany, only 21% of consumers have sufficient financial reserves, while 36% of BNPL users have missed payment deadlines and incurred fees, rising to 47% among users aged 26–39 (SCHUFA, 2025). Additionally, Riverty's brand preference is only 6% at e-commerce checkout (Riverty case study). These figures highlight the opportunity for a Riverty Mastercard offering "Keep it first, pay later" across online and physical stores, with clear repayment visibility and greater control over payment timing.
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Lena is a 32-year-old salaried employee from Cologne with a middling income and little financial cushion who shops primarily on her mobile phone. She values the flexibility of paying only after she has decided to keep a purchase, rather than having money leave her account immediately. However, when shopping in physical stores, Riverty is not available, forcing her to use her debit card and pay upfront, even if she later decides to return the item. This creates unnecessary cash flow pressure and prevents her from having a consistent "keep it first, pay later" experience across all shopping channels.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Enable customers to pay after deciding to keep their purchases, even when shopping in physical stores where Riverty is not currently available.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Encourage existing BNPL users to make Riverty their everyday payment method by enabling "Keep it first, pay later" everywhere. This increases customer retention through more frequent engagement, grows revenue via higher transaction volume, interchange and lending income, and reduces churn by building a direct relationship with consumers beyond merchant checkout.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Active Card Usage Rate-
75% of activated cards used for at least one transaction within 30 days.
Why: Shows early adoption and validates that customers are replacing debit cards with Riverty Mastercard.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** The percentage of customers who successfully complete a purchase using Riverty BNPL at partner merchants should not decrease after the Mastercard launch.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Decision Window: 12 weeks (3 months) after the MVP launch or after reaching the first 500–1,000 activated cardholders, whichever comes first.
Minimum Threshold to Proceed (Scale):
- ≥70% card activation rate among approved users.
- ≥60% of activated users make at least one transaction per month.
- ≥30% of active users complete at least one in-store transaction, validating the "pay later anywhere" proposition.
- Merchant BNPL checkout conversion remains stable (guardrail metric).
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Here's the revised hypothesis from the company's perspective:
Based on qualitative interviews and quantitative evidence showing Riverty's 6% brand preference and that 14% of German BNPL users lose track of outstanding payments, we believe that enabling "Keep it first, pay later" everywhere for Lena—a 32-year-old salaried employee in Cologne with limited financial cushion—will increase everyday Riverty Mastercard usage and strengthen customer loyalty, as measured by an Active Card Usage Rate of at least 75% within 30 days of activation. We will protect the on-time repayment rate (≥98%) and make a go/no-go decision after a 3-month Friends & Family pilot.
